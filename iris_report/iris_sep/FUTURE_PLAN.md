# Future plan: a defensible result with useful operator consequences

## Objective and decision

Help an operator decide whether to escalate human review of a new radiation
storm risk without excessive false warnings. Model a daily NEW >10 MeV,
>=10 pfu crossing within 24 hours. Do not map the threshold directly to damage
or spacecraft protection: mission-specific susceptibility and costs are unknown.

The primary project remains forecast improvement. The admission checks support
it; they do not replace the frozen scientific gate or prove novelty by themselves.
Space companies are intended users. Compare with named reproducible models on
identical data, not with an undefined commercial industry.

## First 7 working days: unblock evidence and fix real defects

1. Reproduce source checks; verify available artifact hashes; replay the failed
   neural checkpoint layer by layer. Write a minimal regression demonstrating
   the actual cause before changing preprocessing or model behavior.
2. Bind admission policy, transform, feature arrays and predictions to the
   verified model/calibration/threshold receipt. Have a separate reviewer design
   hostile tests. Record retained coverage and reasons for abstention.
3. Review any publisher reply supplied by the user. The request was already
   sent; do not resend or send further messages. Require immutable training-only
   data, NEW-crossing/episode semantics, source latency, licensing and an opaque
   evaluation arrangement. Do not inspect locked identities or outcomes.
4. If still blocked, assess primary-source metadata for a separate train-only
   cohort or third-party evaluation service. Before downloading, prove it does
   not overlap protected identities and freeze its protocol. Metadata research
   is allowed; downloading an uncertain cohort is not.

Deliverable: root-cause receipt, independent integrity review, and a written
go/no-go on safe data availability. No new leaderboard tuning during this stage.

## Weeks 2–3: bounded model selection, conditional on verified data

Freeze exact feature names, publication limits, supported sources and class/unit
support for chronological training-side folds. Keep imputation, early stopping,
calibration and operating threshold in distinct inner roles. Use only outer
`train`; previous monitor and inner-score results are already inspected.

Compare fixed climatology, eligible persistence, elastic net, XGBoost, reproduced
SEPNET and compact IRIS. Run a small fixed batch: baseline, causal proton history,
then XRS; connectivity only if authoritative geometry and latency are available.
Five fixed seeds; no architecture sweep. Set budget and all configurations before
execution. Save failed attempts, calibration, thresholds and predictions. Select
the simplest reproducibly useful model, or report no improvement.

Deliverable: a frozen candidate and coherent same-cohort comparison. If data or
comparator fidelity is unresolved, report NOT_RUN rather than fabricate progress.

## Weeks 3–5: decisive evaluation and practical value

Freeze model selection before a single independently controlled evaluation.
Apply the existing complete scientific gate: paired TSS interval above zero,
lower FAR at predeclared matched detection, no material calibration/lead-time
degradation, identical cohort and zero integrity failures. Do not relax it.

Measure warning burden and retained forecast coverage under realistic feed
availability. Compare to credible missing-data baselines and simple guardrails,
not only an unguarded serializer. Any utility model must publish a predeclared
cost sensitivity grid; no invented savings, damage prevented or operator signoff.
External feedback can be drafted but must be explicitly authorized before sending.

Stop/go: if the final gate fails, publish a negative result and request a
separately approved new research direction. Do not spend the remaining schedule
polishing unsupported superiority claims.

## Submission package

Produce a synopsis, cited full paper, receipt-driven figures and a 90-second
student-presented video. The three-minute narrative is supporting material.
Keep a factual student contribution/AI/mentor ledger; students must understand
and defend every methodological choice. Confirm current IRIS/ISEF rules before
submission. The current drafts are not finished submission assets.

Official sources checked 2026-09-05:
- https://iris.exstemplar.com/ — submission components and eligibility.
- https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/ — creativity,
  methodology, interpretation, impact and interview matter.
- https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/ — current rules
  and AI guidance; check again for the actual submission year.

## Twelve-month recognition strategy

Do not build the plan around guaranteeing ten awards. Build one flagship result
with independent review and several legitimate routes for recognition.

- Months 1–2: rigorous experiment and submission package; establish a truthful
  contribution ledger and rehearse the interview.
- Months 3–5: submit to eligible research fairs and student presentation venues;
  verify age/class/location, dates, prior-publication and repeat-submission rules
  on each official site first. Track submissions separately from distinctions.
- Months 6–8: seek authorized external validation; release reproducible source
  and a technical report where appropriate. A release, preprint or presentation
  is a deliverable, not automatically an award or peer-reviewed publication.
- Months 9–12: improve the work using independent findings; enter suitable
  engineering/software competitions and present real adoption outcomes.

Target a researched shortlist of 8–12 eligible opportunities, not ten promised
awards. Do not invent deadlines or eligibility before the student's class, age,
location and intended competition year are known. One strong result with honest
ownership is the central strategy; do not manufacture credentials or count the
same distinction repeatedly.
