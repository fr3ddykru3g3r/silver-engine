# Five hostile judges — questions and reasoning checkpoints

These are diagnostic questions, not scripts to memorize. The student should answer with their own explanation and demonstrate the relevant calculation or evidence.

## Solar/space-weather physicist

1. **Is your SEP definition physically universal?** No; the operational threshold/channel is one convention. Distinguish broad SEP enhancements from threshold-crossing events.
2. **Do >10 MeV and ≥10 MeV mean the same measured thing?** Name the actual integral channel and catalog convention; do not pretend a differential energy bin is the integral flux.
3. **Where did the event start/end times come from?** Show catalog provenance and construction. Agreement with a catalog is not independent raw-flux validation.
4. **Does a daily onset label measure useful lead time?** Not necessarily; demonstrate the issue/horizon relationship. A positive daily target alone does not establish warning lead time or causal input availability.
5. **Is your past-proton proxy an active-state oracle?** No; past-24-hour threshold exceedance and state at issuance differ.
6. **Can two eruptions occur inside one radiation interval?** Yes; the operational episode is not necessarily a single acceleration source. Explain ambiguity and the limits of “physical episode.”
7. **Why trust historical science data for live operation?** Do not claim that. Release times, calibration, quality flags, yaw and sensor mapping must be established.
8. **Could event merging change your answer?** It can change n, r and cohort membership. Alternative definitions remain a robustness gate; the algebra is conditional on the declared partition.

## Statistician

1. **What is the population parameter?** Distinguish a random positive decision window, a uniformly selected represented episode, and eligible onset decisions.
2. **Why is row bootstrap unsuitable?** Rows from an episode share physical conditions and labels; treating them as independent ignores dependence. Explain the actual clustered draws.
3. **Are separate events independent?** Not established. Present calendar-bin sensitivity and remaining solar-cycle nonstationarity.
4. **Did you invent inverse-cluster weighting?** No. Cite the known field after personally reading it; explain the application.
5. **Does all-negative bootstrap imply p<0.0001?** No null distribution was generated. It is a descriptive bootstrap sign fraction.
6. **Did you account for every model tried?** No global selection correction. The diagnostic is conditional on fixed forecasts and historical exposure.
7. **Is an 85-episode estimate independent of model development?** No; sample size and independence are different properties.
8. **Can one detected event validate POD1?** No. Show the wide binomial interval and post-selection limitation.
9. **Why did quiet-block robustness change nothing?** FPR cancels for the principal within-model contrasts; that check cannot test the positive mechanism.
10. **Is Kish concentration your effective sample size?** No; a variance model is missing.

## ML researcher

1. **What changed between evaluation views?** Weights/eligibility only for primary comparisons, not frozen probabilities or thresholds.
2. **Were thresholds chosen on score data?** Show original threshold-role policy; separate subsequent diagnostic multipliers and post-hoc controller development.
3. **Does dropping proton columns prove causation?** No; correlated features, model fitting and selection complicate causal attribution.
4. **Why not deep learning?** Model complexity does not solve an estimand mismatch; the research question is answerable with fixed comparators.
5. **Are features available at issue time?** Nominal historical timestamps are insufficient for harmonized/reprocessed inputs; operational causality remains unproven.
6. **Why is climatology TSS zero?** Constant class decisions give equal sensitivity and FPR; this does not mean its probability Brier score is zero.
7. **Can you compare TSS to a published SOTA table?** Only with compatible target, cohort, source availability and protocol; those equivalences are not established.
8. **Does lower episode Brier mean better calibration?** No; weights and prevalence changed. Explain the two-term Brier decomposition.
9. **Did you rerun the model for verification?** New audit used immutable predictions. A software compatibility fix was tested without retraining frozen models.

## ISEF judge

1. **What did you personally contribute?** Show authentic dated work, not claims based on receiving this package. Separate student, mentor and AI tasks.
2. **Is this just analysis?** Explain a testable question, controlled fixed-prediction comparison, exact mechanism, falsifiable empirical scope and reproducibility. Analytical research can be science; merely retelling literature cannot substitute for execution.
3. **What is creative if the theorem is known?** The application and design may be; demonstrate the literature comparison without claiming priority you cannot establish.
4. **Who benefits?** A model evaluator choosing verification measures for continuing exposure versus new warnings. No measured economic or safety benefit yet.
5. **Explain it in one minute without jargon.** Draw a long event, count repeated positives, then distinguish daily decisions from equal-event averaging.
6. **What is your strongest failure?** Discuss the one-event controller or the failed source-equivalence work and why it was preserved.
7. **Could you reproduce this without AI?** Demonstrate a toy proof and one safe archive calculation; explain the code you used.
8. **Are forms and current-year rules satisfied?** Produce real evidence; do not substitute this workbook for official approval.

## Skeptical reviewer trying to reject it

1. **Your central mathematics is a tautology.** Agree it is an identity; empirical relevance and transparent estimation are the contribution. A new theorem is not claimed.
2. **Your data were already exposed.** Agree; historical mechanistic evidence is not independent prospective performance. This cannot be repaired by renaming a holdout.
3. **You changed the endpoint after seeing the result.** The strict-boundary diagnostic is separately labeled and does not replace the frozen endpoint. Explain the safety restriction and preservation.
4. **Your event definition creates the result.** Partition sensitivity remains a serious limit; do not claim universal robustness before testing a predeclared alternative.
5. **Your model ordering is not significant.** Correct; paired onset comparison includes zero. Keep the decomposition rather than a superiority claim.
6. **You are attacking a paper you did not reproduce.** Restrict criticism to the exact audited table/protocol; do not invalidate separate SEPVAL results.
7. **Your code tests prove only algebra.** They establish specified invariants and reconstruction, not physical correctness or generalization. State those remaining evidence needs.
8. **Your prospective study has already been compromised.** Do not speculate about protected outcomes. Show the boundary conflict and request a custodian governance review without accessing results.
9. **The student is presenting AI work.** Authentic ownership is a gating issue. Disclosure does not turn supplied analysis into independent student discovery; demonstrate actual student work.
10. **Why should I accept it?** The acceptable scope is a reproducible historical estimand audit with exact attribution and candid limits. If the paper claims more, reject those claims.

## Fifteen-minute interrogation drill

Minutes 0–2: question and physical timeline. 2–5: definitions and confusion matrix. 5–8: covariance proof and counterexample. 8–11: bootstrap, exposure and controller failure. 11–13: novelty and operational source limits. 13–15: personal contribution and falsification. The examiner changes one assumption at each stage. Record mistakes and correct reasoning, not a polished memorized answer.

Three largest unresolved scientific weaknesses: development exposure; event-definition/source-causality dependence; unestablished external generalization. Student ownership and eligibility are separate potentially disqualifying gates.
