Status: **HUMAN ACTION REQUIRED — do not mark resolved without a written IRIS reply.**

The current public IRIS guidance states that experimental data cannot exceed 12 months old. The project uses newly constructed labels, splits, experiments, synthetic generators and forecasting analyses, but the underlying public SDO/HMI spacecraft observations span multiple years.

## Exact question to send to IRIS

Subject: Clarification on 12-month data rule for archival astronomy research

Hello IRIS Team,

We are preparing a Physics & Astronomy project that performs new analysis and machine-learning experiments on publicly available NASA SDO/HMI solar magnetograms and NOAA/GOES flare records. Our research work, code, dataset construction, model training and experiments are being conducted during the current IRIS cycle, but some of the underlying spacecraft observations are older than 12 months.

The IRIS guidelines state that “Experimental data cannot exceed 12 months old, though projects may extend previously started work.” Could you please clarify whether this restriction applies to the age of archival public astronomy observations themselves, or to the period in which the student’s own experimental/research work is conducted?

Specifically, may a current-year student project use older public NASA/NOAA observations as an archival scientific dataset if all dataset construction, analysis, experimentation and conclusions are newly performed during the current research cycle?

If there are any additional conditions for archival astronomy datasets, we would appreciate them in writing so we can comply fully.

Thank you.
